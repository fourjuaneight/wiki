**Control**, released in August 2019 by Remedy Entertainment, is a third-person action-adventure game centered on the **Federal Bureau of Control** (FBC), a clandestine U.S. government agency tasked with investigating and containing phenomena that violate the laws of reality.[^controlwp2026] The franchise spans a base game, two downloadable expansions, a cooperative spin-off (*FBC: Firebreak*, 2025), and an upcoming sequel (*Control Resonant*, 2026); it also shares a formal continuity with Remedy's *Alan Wake* series under the umbrella of the **Remedy Connected Universe** (RCU), with the two franchises intersecting at several points in the in-world chronology. This entry traces the full story from the FBC's secretive origins in the 1950s through the Manhattan crisis of *Control Resonant*; Alan Wake lore is covered in depth at every point where it intersects with FBC history, as the two strands become inseparable by the time of the *AWE* expansion.

In-world dates use standard calendar years (CE), consistent with the franchise's real-world setting; a date given in parentheses refers to the real-world release of the installment rather than its in-fiction setting. Approximate in-world dates are marked with `~`. The base game *Control* is set in October 2019, which serves as the timeline's anchor; events preceding it are known primarily through the in-game document archive, and all subsequent installments are placed relative to that anchor.[^controlwiki2026] Note that the *Alan Wake* strand of the RCU predates *Control* in both in-world chronology and real-world release order; its lore is introduced to players primarily through the *AWE* expansion (2020), which retroactively contextualizes events from 2010.

## The proto-Bureau and its early years (~1950s–1964)

The agency that would become the FBC began as a small, unnamed federal office headquartered in Washington, D.C. By 1954, it was "young" in the Bureau's own internal reckoning, holding only three **Altered Items** — mundane objects imbued with paranatural energies from sources outside ordinary reality — and operating with a mandate barely understood even by its own leadership.[^fbcwiki2026] By 1958, the agency had responded to three **Altered World Events** (AWEs), the classification it used for incidents in which paranatural forces breach the boundary between ordinary reality and other dimensions.[^fbcwiki2026] Director **Theodore Ash, Sr.** led the agency through much of this period; his son, Dr. **Theodore Ash, Jr.**, served as Head of Research. The elder Ash held a firm belief in what he called "the esoteric world" and directed the agency's limited resources toward locating and recovering paranatural objects, building an evidence base for phenomena that conventional science could not accommodate.

In 1964, the agency was formally renamed the Federal Bureau of Control, with its cover story framing the reorganization as an initiative to strengthen government relations.[^fbcwiki2026] Following extensive reconnaissance of paranatural locations, the Bureau relocated its headquarters from Washington to a Brutalist skyscraper in New York City known as the **Oldest House**, a **Place of Power** whose interior is geometrically larger than its exterior, which resists detection by anyone not already aware of its existence, and whose architecture shifts unpredictably over time.[^controlwiki2026] One of Ash's senior agents, **Broderick Northmoor**, eventually succeeded him as Director; Northmoor was the first Bureau Director to wield the **Hotline**, an **Object of Power** resembling a Bakelite telephone that spontaneously manifested in the Director's Office and enables communication with the **Board**, a black pyramid-shaped entity residing within the **Astral Plane**.[^controlwiki2026]

Contact with the Board proved transformative in ways that Northmoor's subordinates would not fully understand for decades. Theodore Ash, Jr., in logs later recovered by Jesse Faden, documented how the first expeditions to the Oldest House changed Northmoor irrevocably; the Director restructured the Bureau's mission, culture, and hierarchy to serve the Board's interests rather than the other way around. The nature of those interests remained opaque, but the consequence was an institution that had quietly reoriented itself around a patron it did not fully comprehend.[^controlwp2026]

## The Bright Falls AWE, first contact (~1970–1976)

**Cauldron Lake**, located near the Pacific Northwest town of **Bright Falls, Washington**, drew the Bureau's attention in 1970 when agents confirmed a paranatural incident there; this would be classified as the first in a series of recurring AWEs at the site.[^awewiki2026] Cauldron Lake is a naturally-occurring **Threshold**, a location where the boundary between ordinary reality and other dimensions is thin enough to permit transit; its particular property is that it grants artists the ability to literally rewrite reality through their creative work, making anything committed to manuscript or canvas capable of manifesting in the physical world.

Around 1976, a second wave of activity at Bright Falls surfaced in Bureau records, though agents were not present for the events themselves. Witness accounts described how local musicians **Odin and Tor Anderson** had fought off a malevolent entity their community called "the scratching hag," later identified by the FBC as **Barbara Jagger**, a woman drowned in Cauldron Lake and subsequently possessed by an extradimensional force the Bureau designated the **Dark Presence**.[^awewiki2026] Around the same period, poet and filmmaker **Thomas Zane** disappeared after diving into the lake during an attempt to use its creative power to resurrect Jagger; the Bureau classified a Cauldron Lake AWE in connection with his disappearance.[^controlwiki2026] The FBC designated Bright Falls a recurring AWE site but never stationed agents there long enough to understand the mechanism behind the Dark Presence's influence, a gap that would take decades and two subsequent AWEs to close.

## The Ordinary AWE and the Faden siblings (~2002)

Approximately seventeen years before the events of the main game, an AWE erupted in **Ordinary, Wisconsin**, when two children discovered a slide projector in their neighborhood with unusual properties.[^controlwp2026] The younger of the two siblings, **Dylan Faden**, activated the projector and opened a dimensional portal; something came through. The FBC responded and classified the event as AWE-36; agents recovered the projector, took Dylan into custody, and enrolled him in the **Prime Candidate Program**, the Bureau's project to identify and groom individuals with exceptional paranatural sensitivity as candidates for the Directorship. Dylan was designated **Prime Candidate 6**.[^controlwp2026]

His older sister, **Jesse Faden**, was deliberately left in the outside world as **Prime Candidate 7**, observed at a distance by the Bureau as an informal control group for their experiment.[^controlwp2026] From Jesse's perspective the Bureau had simply abducted her brother; she carried a connection to a paranatural entity she perceived as an inner voice and guiding light, naming it **Polaris**, and spent the next seventeen years trying to find Dylan. The Bureau knew Polaris under a different designation, Hedron; it was a physical crystalline entity that had been recovered and contained within the Oldest House, and its resonant frequency offered protection against paranatural influence. The Bureau's decision to leave Jesse outside, rather than bring both children in, would have consequences neither side could anticipate.

## The Bright Falls AWE, second incident (2010)

### Alan Wake and the Dark Place

In 2010, bestselling thriller writer **Alan Wake** and his wife **Alice** traveled to Bright Falls for a working retreat, hoping to break a severe creative block. The trip ended catastrophically when the Dark Presence targeted Alice, using Cauldron Lake's power to abduct her. In desperation, Wake attempted to exploit the lake's reality-bending properties himself: using his ability to alter the world through his writing, he composed a manuscript intended to save Alice, not yet understanding that the Dark Place uses narrative logic as the mechanism of its own perpetuation.[^awewiki2026] Alice was freed, but the story Wake wrote had consequences he had not anticipated; he was pulled beneath the lake and into the **Dark Place**, the same nightmarish dimension where Thomas Zane had been trapped decades earlier. In the Dark Place, thoughts and writings can manifest as reality, escape attempts generate new narrative loops, and time does not pass normally; Wake found himself trapped in a prison that seemed designed to prevent any exit he could write.

The FBC responded to the Bright Falls events under the designation **AWE-47** and acquired all research materials connected to Dr. **Emil Hartman**, the psychiatrist who had operated the Cauldron Lake Lodge.[^awewiki2026] Hartman had spent years studying the lake and its connection to creativity, running a clinic that housed troubled artists he believed could be channeled as instruments of the lake's power. In 2010, the Dark Presence had possessed Hartman directly; agents extracted him and contained him in the Oldest House's **Investigations Sector**, a sealed wing used to house the most sensitive AWE evidence and detainees. The sector was subsequently closed after Hartman, now designated "Shade Hartman," breached containment and spread a darkness-based entity through the wing, trapping anyone inside. The FBC sealed the Investigations Sector and left its remaining occupants to their fate.

From within the Dark Place, Wake discovered that the FBC's Hotline could carry transmissions out of the dimension, and began sending messages to the Oldest House. The Board's connection to the Hotline, and the Hotline's connection to the Director, meant that whoever held the Directorship would eventually receive Wake's signals. He spent years attempting to write his way out of the Dark Place and, when direct escape proved impossible, began attempting to write someone else into a position where they could help him.

## Director Trench and the approach of the Hiss (~2010–2019)

**Zachariah Trench** served as Director of the FBC for the decade between the Bright Falls AWE and the events of the main game. Head of Research **Dr. Casper Darling** became one of the Bureau's most consequential figures during this period, leading expeditions from the Oldest House into paranatural dimensions and making contact with Polaris/Hedron, which he recognized as a genuinely benevolent entity rather than a threat.[^controlwp2026] Darling developed **Hedron Resonance Amplifiers** (HRAs), devices capable of shielding individuals from paranatural possession, and began distributing them to key Bureau staff; he was sufficiently alarmed by the things he had seen on expeditions that he wanted protection in place before anything went wrong.

Trench had already been compromised. During one of Darling's expeditions, Trench had retained a burned slide from a slide projector; the burned slide was a doorway to an entity called the **Hiss**, an aggressive paranatural force transmitted on a resonant frequency that could possess any individual not shielded by an HRA.[^controlwp2026] The Hiss worked on Trench slowly, exploiting his anxieties about Darling's growing influence and his perceived threat from Hedron's protective resonance. When Darling distributed the HRAs to Bureau staff, Trench interpreted it as preparation for a takeover by Hedron, not protection against a threat. Under the Hiss's influence, he inserted the burned slide into the Oldest House's slide projector, opening a conduit for the Hiss to invade. Shortly before Jesse Faden's arrival, Trench shot himself in his Director's Office, becoming the first casualty of the invasion he had triggered.

## The Hiss invasion (October 2019)

Jesse Faden arrived at the Oldest House in October 2019 following a telepathic summons from Polaris, seeking her brother.[^controlwp2026] She found the building in lockdown, Trench's body in the Director's Office, and the **Service Weapon** — a modular **Object of Power** that shapeshifts into multiple firearm configurations — on the floor beside him. Acting on Polaris's guidance, Jesse picked it up and was immediately transported to the Astral Plane, where the Board appointed her Director of the FBC. She emerged to find the building overrun by Hiss-possessed agents and the few survivors clustered around HRAs they could not fully understand.

Jesse worked her way through the Oldest House's four primary sectors; Executive, Maintenance, Research, and Containment; clearing the Hiss, restoring communications, and unlocking new paranatural abilities through Objects of Power she located throughout the building. She eventually found Dylan in the Containment Sector and, rather than a prisoner waiting for rescue, encountered a young man who had embraced the Hiss as an instrument of revenge against a Bureau that had held him captive since childhood. Dylan attempted to use the Hiss to seize control of the Board itself. Jesse confronted him in the Astral Plane and cleansed him; his residual connection to Polaris from the Ordinary AWE prevented the cleansing from killing him, but he fell into a deep coma.[^controlwp2026]

With the Slide Projector destroyed and the Hiss severed from its primary point of entry, Jesse accepted the Directorship and turned to clearing the building's remaining Hiss contamination. The fake-out ending sequence Jesse experiences in the Astral Plane; a bureaucratic time loop orchestrated by the Hiss; makes clear what accepting the role actually means. She resists the illusion, becomes the true resonance point for Polaris now that Hedron has been destroyed, and closes the dimensional conduit.[^controlwp2026]

## The Nail crisis (late 2019)

Shortly after the Hiss invasion, the Board contacted Jesse through the Hotline with an urgent summons to the **Foundation**, a vast cave system running beneath the Oldest House that had never been fully mapped.[^foundwiki2026] The **Nail**, an obsidian monolith buried in the Foundation's deepest reaches that connects the Oldest House to the Astral Plane and is the structural source of the Oldest House's paranatural properties, had been severely damaged; the Astral Plane was bleeding through into the Foundation's caverns, and if the process was not stopped, both dimensions faced destruction.

Jesse also needed to find **Helen Marshall**, the Bureau's Head of Operations, who had entered the Foundation during the initial Hiss invasion and gone missing. The Board granted Jesse two new abilities; Shape, the power to grow crystal platforms from certain surfaces, and Fracture, the power to destroy crystal barriers; to navigate the crystalline terrain the Astral Bleed had generated. She also encountered the **Former**, an extradimensional entity that claimed to have been a member of the Board before being exiled for an undisclosed transgression; the Former provided tactical support against the Hiss forces in the Foundation while maintaining its own opaque agenda.[^foundwiki2026]

Jesse located Marshall at the base of the Nail; Marshall had been recently possessed by the Hiss. After a fierce fight, Jesse killed her and cleansed the Nail. What she learned afterward was as unsettling as the crisis itself: Marshall had deliberately damaged the Nail to prevent both the Hiss and the Board from using it as a lever over the Bureau. The Board had responded by remotely destroying Marshall's HRA, leaving her unprotected and ensuring her possession. Jesse, with her trust in the Board shattered, resolved to lead the Bureau independently; she would allow the Board to believe it remained in control while pursuing her own judgment. Marshall had counted on exactly that outcome.[^foundwiki2026]

## The Investigations Sector (~late 2019–early 2020)

Alan Wake's transmissions through the Hotline had directed Jesse to the sealed Investigations Sector, guiding her toward the Bright Falls AWE evidence archive and toward Hartman.[^awewiki2026] During the Hiss invasion, the Hiss had entered the sealed sector and merged with the Dark Presence entity possessing Hartman; the two paranatural forces amplified one another, creating a hybrid entity of considerably greater destructiveness than either would have been alone. The sector was in a state of active contamination, darkness spreading from Hartman's location and Hiss corruption spreading everywhere else.

Jesse made her way through the sector, excavating the Bureau's full documented history with Bright Falls; the Thomas Zane disappearance, the Anderson brothers, the AWE classification of Alan Wake's experience, Alice Wake's 2017 visit to the Oldest House for a Bureau interview about her missing husband, and the series of manuscript pages Wake had managed to transmit from the Dark Place.[^awewiki2026] She killed Hartman and cleared the sector. Wake's transmissions through the investigation made his intentions increasingly clear: he had been orchestrating events from the Dark Place, using his ability to shape narrative reality to write Jesse into a position where she might eventually be able to reach him. Whether the Bureau itself, the Hiss, and everything Jesse had been through were partly his creation remained deliberately unanswered.

The closing moments of the operation carried an immediate and unsettling coda. Frederick Langston, the Bureau's Panopticon supervisor, informed Jesse that the FBC had just received an AWE report from Bright Falls; but the timestamp on the signal was dated several years in the future.[^awewiki2026] Jesse suggested the data might be accurate if the AWE had already been underway before the Oldest House lockdown blocked the signal's arrival. Whether she was right, and what that AWE would become, would not be clear for years.

## The dark years: Oldest House lockdown (~2020–2024)

The Oldest House remained under active Hiss contamination in the years following the invasion, severed from routine FBC operations by a lockdown that grew increasingly difficult to explain or maintain. No contact came from the building for approximately four years; the FBC's broader apparatus continued to function, but the Oldest House itself effectively went dark.[^lakehousegs2024] Dylan remained in his containment cell in a coma that gradually lightened, and Jesse's whereabouts in this period remain unresolved; she is absent from all confirmed records.

The AWE report from Bright Falls eventually came due. In approximately 2023 — thirteen years after Alan Wake's disappearance — a string of ritualistic murders brought the FBI to Bright Falls, where Agent Saga Anderson's investigation intersected with the FBC's ongoing monitoring of the site. The FBC despatched Agent **Kiran Estevez** to investigate the **Lake House**, a Bureau research facility outside Bright Falls that had been conducting unauthorized experiments during the communication blackout, exploiting the Oldest House's silence to run tests that would never have been sanctioned.[^lakehousegs2024]

During her investigation, Estevez was transported via the **Oceanview Motel**, a paranatural transit space that functions as a threshold between dimensions and connects several points within the RCU, to a section of the Oldest House's Panopticon.[^lakehousegs2024] There she encountered Dylan Faden; no longer catatonic, awake in his cell, with years of captivity written clearly on him. Dylan told Estevez that something was "changing outside" and asked her to tell Jesse he had tried. What followed was a cascade of visions: Manhattan's streets warped and folded by paranatural forces, the Hiss spreading far beyond the Oldest House, anomalous **Mold** blooming through the city, and a new resonance distorting reality itself.[^lakehousegs2024] Estevez was ejected back to the Lake House without further explanation. The encounter confirmed that the Oldest House's containment perimeter was failing, and that whatever had been sealed inside the building was no longer willing to stay.

## FBC: Firebreak and the boiling point (2025)

By 2025, approximately six years after the initial Hiss invasion, the Oldest House remained under active siege and had reached what internal FBC communications described as a "boiling point."[^firebreakwiki2026] Standard containment protocols had proven insufficient against a Hiss presence that had had years to entrench itself. The Bureau established **Firebreak**, its most adaptable response unit, composed of volunteers drawn from across the FBC's ranks; combat-trained personnel, administrative staff, secretaries, and middle management; all equipped with non-standard weapons and **paranatural augments** derived from weaponized Altered Items, and deployed in three-person units into isolated sectors of the Oldest House.[^firebreakwiki2026]

Firebreak operations were targeted interventions rather than a coordinated campaign to retake the building. Teams were sent into high-danger areas to suppress acute Hiss incursions, neutralize destabilized Altered Items, and prevent local containment collapses from cascading into larger breaches. The Bureau's underlying concern, confirmed by the Lake House incident, was not simply that the Hiss remained inside the Oldest House; it was that the boundary between the Oldest House and the wider world was dissolving faster than any intervention could offset.[^firebreakwp2026] Firebreak was, in the Bureau's own framing, a firebreak in the ecological sense: a barrier to stop the spread of something already burning, not an attempt to put the fire out.

*FBC: Firebreak* was released in June 2025 and received mixed critical reviews. Remedy announced the cessation of new content development in March 2026 and redirected resources toward narrative-driven projects, while pledging to maintain game servers for the foreseeable future.[^firebreakwp2026]

## The Manhattan crisis (~2026)

The failure Firebreak was designed to forestall eventually arrived: the paranatural forces contained within the Oldest House; the Hiss, the Mold, and a newly identified phenomenon the FBC designates the **Pattern**, a third resonance force distinct from both; escaped containment and spilled into downtown Manhattan.[^resonantwiki2026] The city's streets became a paranaturally altered landscape; impossible architecture crystallized mid-block, realities shifted without warning, and entities that should never have existed outside Bureau walls moved freely through a city that could not explain what was happening to it. Jesse Faden, still Director in title, went missing; her whereabouts and the circumstances of her departure are unconfirmed.[^resonantwp2026]

Facing a crisis that its standard operational posture could not meet, the Bureau released Dylan Faden from his years-long captivity in the Oldest House and deployed him as an active field operative into the altered city.[^resonantwp2026] He is equipped with the **Aberrant**, a shapeshifting weapon of paranatural derivation capable of taking multiple melee forms; hammers, scythes, and configurations that have no real-world analogue; as well as the full range of telekinetic and paranatural abilities he developed during his years as a Prime Candidate and through his connection to the Hiss. Dylan's mission is dual: to confront the Pattern and the other forces now loose in Manhattan, and to find his sister, whose disappearance shapes every aspect of the crisis he is being asked to resolve.[^resonantwp2026]

*Control Resonant* is scheduled for worldwide release on September 24, 2026.[^resonantwp2026]
