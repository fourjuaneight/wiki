The **Pentax MX** is a fully mechanical 35 mm single-lens reflex camera, built by Asahi Optical from 1976 to 1985. It was the Pentax flagship until the LX arrived, and it is internally a smaller, lighter version of the earlier KX.[^wikipediand] Paired with it here is the **SMC Pentax 50 mm f/1.4**, engraved *SMC PENTAX 1:1.4/50*, the first bayonet-mount version of the Pentax fast standard prime.[^pentaxforumsnd]

The two are close in age but not a factory pair. The lens belongs to the 1975 K series; the MX shipped a year later with the smaller M series. Both use the same K mount, so the combination works with no adapter and no loss of function. This entry covers the mechanism of each, the way the pair behaves in use, and a working exposure guide.

## The K mount and the compact turn

Until 1975, Pentax SLRs used the M42 screw mount. The K bayonet replaced it that year. A bayonet aligns the lens in one fixed rotational position, which lets the body read the aperture and the lens read the body — impossible on a screw thread, where the lens stops wherever the threads end.

At the same time, the Olympus OM-1 had made the full-size SLR body look bulky. The first K-series bodies were large. Pentax answered in 1976 with the **ME** and the MX, and with the **M series** of lenses, which cut roughly 20 % of the length and weight of their K-series equivalents.[^lensdbnd] The MX took the mechanical, manual path; the ME took the automatic one.

The K-series 50 mm f/1.4 was caught in this change. It ran for two years, from 1975 to 1977, before the M version replaced it.[^pentaxforumsnd] Its short life is why it is less common than the M version, and why measured test data for it is thin.

## The MX body

The shutter is a horizontal-run cloth focal-plane type, mechanically timed from 1 s to 1/1000 s, plus B. Flash synchronises at 1/60 s.[^camerawikind] Because the timing is mechanical, the camera works with no battery. The two LR44 cells drive the meter only.[^analogcafe2025]

Metering is TTL, open-aperture, centre-weighted, and read from five LEDs in the viewfinder: a green centre LED for correct exposure, two orange LEDs for a half-stop error, and two red LEDs for one stop or more.[^camerawikind] The film-speed dial accepts ISO 32 to 1600.[^imagingpixelnd]

The viewfinder is the reason many people keep this camera. Magnification is 0.97x with 95 % coverage, which is close to life-size and unusually large for a body this small.[^camerawikind] The screen is interchangeable through the mount, with eight options that are shared with the LX.

## The 50 mm f/1.4

The optical formula is 7 elements in 6 groups, carried over from the M42-mount SMC Takumar 50 mm f/1.4.[^pentaxforumsnd] The K version is not a new design. It is the Takumar optics in a new barrel, made larger because the K mount has a wider inner diameter than the screw mount it replaced.

The diaphragm has 8 blades and closes to f/22. Minimum focus is 45 cm, for 0.15x magnification. The filter thread is 52 mm. The lens measures 63 × 42 mm and weighs 265 g — larger and heavier than the M version that followed it, which uses a 49 mm thread and weighs 236 g.[^pentaxforumsnd][^riley2024] There is no *A* setting on the aperture ring, so the lens gives manual and aperture-priority operation only on later bodies. On the MX this makes no difference, because the MX has no automatic mode.

Optically the lens changes character at f/2. Wide open it shows low contrast, clear vignetting, visible chromatic aberration, and soft edges. At f/2 all four improve sharply. From f/2.8 to f/5.6 it behaves like any good standard prime.[^pentaxforumsnd] Measurements of the closely related M version put central sharpness as excellent from f/2 to f/16, and edge sharpness as very good from f/2.8 to f/16.[^riley2024]

### Thoriated glass

Many copies of this lens contain thorium oxide in the rear element. Thorium raises the refractive index of glass while keeping dispersion low, which let designers of the period correct aberrations with fewer elements. The cost is that thorium is radioactive, and its decay slowly browns the glass.

The result is a yellow cast that grows over decades. It is real and it will shift colour film. It is also reversible: extended exposure to ultraviolet light clears the glass.[^pentaxforumsnd] Black-and-white work is largely unaffected. Colour negative film shows the cast, though scanning correction removes most of it.

## Exposure in practice

The tables below assume Kodak 200 film. They use two-stop steps, so each row does a different job.

### Aperture

| Aperture | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| f/1.4 | Bokeh | Sharp centre, soft edges, heavy vignetting. |
| f/2.8 | Portrait | The edges recover here. |
| f/5.6 | Group | Two or three rows of people stay sharp. |
| f/8 | Landscape | Best all-round aperture. Focus at 10.4 m (34 ft) for sharpness from 5.2 m (17 ft) to infinity. |
| f/11 | People + background | Focus at 7.6 m (25 ft) for sharpness from 3.8 m (12 ft) to infinity. |

f/16 and f/22 are on the ring. Use f/16 only for a very close foreground. Use f/22 only for an 8-point sunstar.

The reason f/8 wins is diffraction. Closing the aperture reduces the lens aberrations but spreads each point of light wider, so a best aperture exists between the two effects rather than at the end of the scale.[^atkinsnd]

### Shutter speed

Set the film-speed dial to ISO 200.

| Speed | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | Night, light trails | Tripod only. |
| 1/4 | Dim interior, deliberate blur | Tripod or a braced body. |
| 1/15 | Low light, panning | Below the handheld limit. Expect shake. |
| 1/60 | Handheld standard | Flash sync speed. Handheld limit for a 50 mm lens. |
| 1/250 | People walking, street | Bright sun: pair with f/16 (Sunny 16). |
| 1/1000 | Fast action | Top speed. Bright sun: pair with f/8. |

In bright sun the camera cannot go wider than f/8. Fit a neutral density filter or wait for softer light. This is the one real limit of a mechanical body with a 1/1000 s ceiling and a fast lens: the aperture the lens was bought for is unusable in daylight without help.

## Buying and condition notes

Two faults are common on the K-series 50 mm f/1.4. The first is the thorium yellowing above, which is fixable. The second is balsam separation, where the cement between glued elements fails and produces a fuzzy or swirling blur. Separation is not repairable in practice, and it worsens with age. Japanese sellers usually disclose it by name; others often do not.[^pentaxforumsnd]

On the body, check the shutter at 1 s and at 1/1000 s, since a mechanical shutter that has sat unused drifts slowest at the extremes. The meter is the only electrical part, so a dead meter does not stop the camera from working.
